J-Chess

Play Info

Rules

The rules of J-Chess are simple. All standard chess rules apply except:

  • There is no castling.
  • The back rank is randomised independently for both sides.
  • The king does not need to start between the rooks.

How Many Positions?

One side has 2,880 legal back-rank arrangements. Randomising both sides independently yields 2,880 × 2,880 = 8,294,400 possible starting positions.

For a bit of added fun, that means if you press the J-Chess button, there is a 1 in 2,880 chance the position will be symmetrical. Also a 1 in 8,294,400 chance you get the standard starting position and if you press Symmetrical J-Chess then a 1 in 2,880 chance of getting the standard starting position (without castling of course).

Fair Positions

A sample of 10,000 random starts was analysed (Stockfish 17.1, 1 second each). This resulted in about 22 % of positions falling within ±0.40 pawns, so roughly 1.8 million of all positions are “fair”. The Fair J-Chess button keeps sampling and briefly evaluating positions until it hits one in that band. With ~22 % fair you usually get a balanced start within a handful of tries.

Why I Made This

I’m not keen on deep opening memorisation. I prefer pattern recognition that you can use in completely different positions. Chess960 helps, but you can still prepare a modest subset even lightly. If it is possible, the top players will do it. Castling is also quite complicated for beginners to learn.

Here the sheer number of balanced starting positions makes prep pointless. Dropping castling and independently randomising each back rank boosts variety further. Independent randomising makes sure that the kings don’t always face each other. Without castling this would result in lack of opposite-side castling-like positions. With independent randomising, the kings can be in completely different locations or both tucked away safely.

Each side has different pros and cons. That’s what I like about J-Chess. You never know what position you’ll get. You have to make the most of what you’ve been given, knowing that the starting setup is roughly equal.
