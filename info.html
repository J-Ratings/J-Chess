What Is J-Chess?

J-Chess (Jumble Chess or Jukes Chess) is a chess variant where each side’s back rank is randomly arranged (bishops start on opposite colours). It is similar in spirit to Chess960, but castling is not a rule and so the king does not need to be between the rooks. This variant uses independent randomisation for both White and Black – so the two back ranks need not mirror each other, producing far more distinct starting positions.

How Many Positions?

For one side, there are 2,880 possible starting positions. Doing the randomising for both sides independently gives 2,880 x 2,880 = 8,294,400. So there are over 8 million possible starting positions in J-Chess.

Fair Positions

A sample of 10,000 positions were analysed with Stockfish 17.1 at 1 second per position using Intel i9 hardware. 2,189 positions were not outside the +/- 0.4 threshold, so about 22% of positions are fair. Scaling up, that's 1,815,644 fair positions in total - nearly 2 million.

The “Generate Fair Position” button generates a random J-Chess position and runs a short Stockfish evaluation. If the eval is outside +/- 0.4 then it repeats the process with a new J-Chess position until a fair evaluation is found. Given about 22% of positions are 'fair', there will be roughly 50% chance you will get a fair position in 3 iterations and over 90% chance you will get a fair position in 10 iterations.

Why I Made This

As much as I enjoy chess, I have a great dislike for deep opening prep. Chess960 is certainly a step in the right direction, however, it is not impossible to deeply analyse 10-20 positions and hope they come up. Or perhaps analyse lightly 100 positions. If it's possible, the top players will do it. With J-Chess, there are nearly 2 million fair positions. No chance of top players memorising.

J-Chess also has no castling which I see as a plus. The king does not necessarily get to safety leading to dynamic creative positions. It also increases the number of possible positions for one side from 960 => 2880.

A reason I felt it necessary to not have symetrical positions (apart from greater combinations) is that no castling + symmetry leads to kings always being opposite each other for most of the game. This can remove the thrill of opposite-side castling battles. With independent shuffling, you might get opposite side kings or you might get a position where both kings are safe. You never know! You work with what you've got, safe in the knowledge that the position is equal.
