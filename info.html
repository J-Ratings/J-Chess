Jumble Chess – Info

Play | Info

What Is Jumble Chess?

Jumble Chess is a casual variant where each side’s back rank is randomly arranged (subject to bishops starting on opposite colours) while the pawns remain in their usual places. It is similar in spirit to Chess960, but uses an independent randomisation for Black as well as White – so the two back ranks need not mirror each other, producing far more distinct starting positions.

How Many Positions?

There are 8! / 4 = 10,080 legal bishop‑opposite layouts for one side (divide by 2 for swapping the two bishops’ colour squares and divide by 2 again for king between rooks constraint not being enforced here). Because White and Black are generated independently, the total number of raw starting pairs is 10,080 × 10,080 ≈ 101.6 million. The playable subset used here is filtered, giving about 8.3 million distinct starting positions after removing duplicates arising from symmetry and simple invalid configurations. A further fairness filter (engine evaluation in a narrow centipawn window) yields roughly 2.5 million “fair” starts.

(Those figures are empirical / implementation‑specific rather than a pure combinatorial identity, hence “about”.)

Fair Positions

The “Generate Fair Position” button repeatedly samples random starts and runs a short Stockfish evaluation (configurable time) until it finds a position with an evaluation whose absolute value is at most 0.40 pawns. This screens out severely imbalanced setups (e.g. a trapped rook or an exposed king) while keeping variety.

The fairness check uses: go movetime <X> with a lightweight build of Stockfish. A position is accepted if |score| ≤ 40 centipawns.

Playing Strength Levels

The engine responds using a MultiPV search (10 candidate lines). After a search capped by a node budget that grows with the selected level, it gathers the candidate moves and introduces randomness: any move whose score is within a level‑dependent threshold (centipawns) of the top move can be chosen. Early in the game (first few ply groups) the threshold is temporarily halved at intermediate levels to avoid gifting material too quickly while still feeling human. Higher levels shrink the threshold and raise the node count.

Why I Made It

Standard openings can become rote. By jumbling both sides independently you get fresh tactical patterns every game; you must construct a plan from scratch rather than recall theory. The fairness filter keeps games competitive instead of starting from a decisive plus or minus. It is also a convenient testbed for experimenting with engine “humanisation” via MultiPV sampling and strength scaling by node limits plus a half‑threshold mechanism.

Example FEN

rnqbkbnr/pppppppp/8/8/8/8/PPPPPPPP/RNBQKBNR w - - 0 1

Format: standard Forsyth–Edwards; only the back ranks change. Castling rights are deliberately stripped (- -) because the king and rooks may be anywhere; implementing variant castling rules is out of scope for this minimal version.

Limitations / Future Ideas

  • Per‑position seeding so a position can be shared by short code.
  • Optional symmetry mode (same shuffle for both sides) for a Chess960‑like feel.
  • Time control and move list export (PGN).
  • Better strength calibration (Elo mapping is only approximate).
