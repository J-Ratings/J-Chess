What Is Jumble Chess?

Jumble Chess is a chess variant where each side’s back rank is randomly arranged (subject to bishops starting on opposite colours) while the pawns remain in their usual places. It is similar in spirit to Chess960, but uses an independent randomisation for Black as well as White – so the two back ranks need not mirror each other, producing far more distinct starting positions. There is also no castling involved.

How Many Positions?

Fair Positions

The “Generate Fair Position” button generates a random J-Chess position and runs a short Stockfish evaluation. If the eval is outside +/- 0.4 then it repeats the process with a new J-Chess position until a fair evaluation is found. Given about 30% of positions are 'fair', the chance of waiting more than 10 positions is is <3%.

Why I Made It

I have a great dislike for deep opening prep. Chess960 is certainly a step in the right direction but I find the castling rules fiddly. I also think that no castling leads to more interesting positions. In Chess960, it is not impossible to deeply analyse 10-20 positions and hope they come up. Or perhaps analyse

J-Chess removes all hope for opening prep. As there is no castling, the king does not need to be between the rooks giving more options. However, by removing castling, we do introduce a more restrictive style such as no opposite side castling positions. To counter this, I have forced the jumbling to occur independently for both sides. Now the kings can sometimes be on opposite sides. There are also now millions of positions completely removing any opportunity to prep.
